# Threat Model

## System Positioning

This project is a reference prototype for institutional cross-chain collateral and lending on two permissioned Besu/QBFT ledgers. It uses quorum-signed checkpoints and EVM account/storage proofs to authorize asynchronous cross-chain execution.

It is not a trustless public bridge, not a full IBC implementation, not audited, and not production-ready.

## Protected Assets and Properties

- Canonical aBANK held in Bank A escrow.
- Voucher vA supply and the one-to-one unsettled collateral liability.
- bCASH liquidity, borrower debt, reserves, and lending solvency state.
- Message commitments, receipts, acknowledgements, refunds, and terminal states.
- Attestor, validator, relayer, governance, identity, oracle, and operator keys.
- Defense-evidence reports, their source applicability, and the separation between public reports and secret runtime artifacts.
- Safety properties: proof validity, route integrity, replay resistance, conservation, policy enforcement, and governance delay.
- Liveness properties: block production, checkpoint availability, relay progress, acknowledgement, and timeout recovery.

## Trust Assumptions

| Component | Trust assumption | Principal risk | Implemented control / remaining boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Besu validators | Each bank operates a governed 4-validator QBFT network. | Collusion, network partition, or loss of quorum. | The profile tolerates one unavailable validator; Byzantine injection and cross-bank infrastructure independence require further validation. |
| Attestors | Fewer than 3 of 4 attestors are malicious or compromised for false-root resistance; at most one is Byzantine for quorum-intersection consistency; at least three remain responsive for liveness. | A malicious quorum can attest an arbitrary root. | EIP-712 domain binding, destination-domain allowlisting, signer ordering, epoch/quorum checks, maximum submission age, conflict freeze, and durable equivocation guards. Production keys require separate HSM-backed operators. |
| Checkpoint governance | Timelock governance rotates attestors and recovers frozen clients only after institutional review. | Malicious or mistaken signer/route replacement. | Recovery raises an authorization floor so pre-recovery roots remain auditable but cannot authorize proofs; sensitive roles are transferred to timelocks. Production proposers/executors must be multisig-controlled. |
| Relayer | Relayers may fail, duplicate, delay, or reorder work. | Censorship and delayed settlement. | Relayers cannot create a quorum or bypass proof checks. Durable jobs, reconciliation, bounded retries, lease heartbeats/fencing tokens and on-chain receipts support recovery; the local JSON journal permits one process owner only. |
| EVM proof verifier | MPT/RLP verification and storage-slot derivation are correct. | A parser or boundary bug may accept invalid state. | Repository assurance adds canonical-RLP/hex-prefix rejection, malformed/mutated fuzzing, a separately implemented reference walker and a deterministic offline corpus generated by pinned EthereumJS Trie. Phase 9 integration v3, when executed, additionally requires Besu identity on both chains and retains four raw `eth_getProof` observations only after the production gateway accepts them. The offline corpus itself keeps `validatedLiveClients=[]`; the live report covers one client family, not a formal proof, multi-client cross-suite or independent audit. |
| Gateway routes | Governance configures the intended remote gateway and versioned applications. | Misrouting, wrong-chain deployment, cross-asset velocity-accounting drift, premature old-route revocation, stale migration trust, or privileged abuse. | Constructor chain-ID/code guards, `(account, asset, day)` limits, chain/gateway/application binding, seven-day maximum transfer lifetime, matching route drain, pending-message count, explicit old-endpoint revocation, pause, replay receipts, and mutually exclusive terminal states. |
| Identity and policy | Bank KYC/compliance systems issue correct on-chain eligibility and limits. | Fraudulent credential, stale status, or unsafe cap. | Expiry/status checks, terminal revocation, role separation, allowlists, per-account and asset caps, exposure accounting, and guardian suspension. Off-chain KYC correctness remains trusted. |
| Restitution governance | The timelock adjudicates restricted claims and selects a currently eligible recipient. | Wrongful or delayed release of a revoked customer's restricted assets. | Terminal-revocation timeout assets enter an accounted vault; release requires `CLAIM_ADMIN_ROLE`, a nonzero case reference, active identity and current bank-policy approval. |
| Oracle | Authorized operators publish accurate and timely prices. | Manipulated or stale valuation can create bad debt. | Timestamped prices, staleness checks, risk caps, liquidation, reserve accounting, and pause. The local manual oracle is not a production oracle network. |
| Lending contracts | Financial parameters reflect the bank's risk policy. | Insolvency from parameter error, unit mismatch, forgiven accrual time, unsafe recapitalization/liquidation, or lending again after default. | The current market explicitly rejects non-18-decimal tokens, requires contract bytecode for dependencies/oracle, invalidates zero-asset supplier shares through a loss epoch before recapitalization, and retains granular pause, accrual backlog, origination-principal/default, liquidation, reserve and bad-debt controls. Economic modeling remains simplified and arbitrary-decimal markets require a separate design. |
| UI/runtime service | The presentation service submits user actions and reads chain state. | Duplicate action after an ambiguous response, browser CSRF, stale display, or unavailable interface. | Exact-raw signed-transaction outbox, retained request IDs, same-origin CSRF controls, bounded RPC requests and owned-resource cleanup reduce the local runtime surface. It holds no proof-validation authority; contracts remain the source of truth. Local generated accounts are demonstration credentials only. |
| Evidence host and toolchain | The operating system, filesystem, Git, Node/npm, Hardhat and Docker/Besu installation execute and store results faithfully. | A compromised host/toolchain can forge executions, reports, source state or checksums; concurrent runner instances or mid-run source changes can produce a mixed bundle. | Explicit environment allowlist, cooperative global runner lock, stable initial/completion provenance, exact-signature structured results, exact deployed-contract inventory, report/profile checksums and current-source applicability detect accidental or untrusted-input inconsistency. Git lookup and symlinked provenance inputs fail closed. No hostile-process isolation, independent signed attestation or hostile-host resistance is claimed. |

## Adversary Capabilities Considered

- Submit malformed, stale, wrong-domain, duplicate-signer, insufficient-quorum, or conflicting checkpoints, including historical evidence outside the automatic conflict path.
- Relay a valid proof with the wrong account, storage slot, value, route, application, nonce, payload, or timeout.
- Repeat delivery, acknowledgement, or timeout transactions before and after a relayer restart.
- Stop one validator, one attestor, or the relayer process.
- Attempt completion after refund, refund after completion, or execution after receipt insertion.
- Use expired, suspended, revoked, disallowed, over-cap, or undercollateralized customer state.
- Trigger stale-price, illiquid, liquidation, and bad-debt paths.
- Reuse an action request identifier after an ambiguous UI timeout or process restart.
- Transfer a collateral voucher directly between customer wallets to bypass the approved lending route.
- Inject unsafe runtime/profile/process variables, substitute a security test through ambiguous name matching, remove required report content, alter a component report, or present evidence from a different/dirty source revision.
- Start concurrent evidence-runner instances, change source or tool provenance during a run, add/remove a deployed-bytecode entry, or leave an ambiguous lock after interruption.

## Enforced On-Chain

- Checkpoint signer membership, threshold, epoch, destination domain, maximum submission age, clock drift, monotonic height, in-window conflict freeze, and post-recovery authorization floor. Accepted roots have no automatic TTL; conflicts outside the submission-age or current/previous-epoch window require guardian freeze and governed recovery.
- EVM account/storage membership and absence proofs under an accepted state root.
- Exact message commitment, trusted gateway, application route, receipt, acknowledgement, timeout, and replay checks.
- Escrow/voucher conservation and application compensation callbacks.
- Unique client references at the origin application, message receipts at the destination, and mutually exclusive terminal states.
- Identity eligibility, policy allowlists, caps, exposure accounting, and emergency pause.
- Operator-restricted voucher transfers and terminal credential revocation.
- Accounted restricted restitution, timelocked claim administration, adjudication reference, and policy-checked release.
- Oracle freshness, borrowing capacity, liquidity, interest, liquidation, reserve, and bad-debt accounting.
- Timelocked administrative ownership after bootstrap.

## Off-Chain but Security-Relevant

- Attestors independently read finalized source blocks, reject destinations outside their configured `(chainId, checkpointClient)` allowlist, and persist an equivocation guard before signing.
- Relayers collect signatures, construct EIP-1186 proofs, persist jobs, retry, and reconcile with on-chain state.
- Before a financial UI broadcast, action-journal v3 persists the exact signed raw transaction and binds it to request/action/amount/lane, chain, signer and nonce. Startup/status recovery checks its exact hash and may rebroadcast only those same bytes. Any unresolved financial action globally fences new local-runtime actions before prerequisites, and ambiguous nonce handling never advances the business call to a new nonce.
- Validators, attestors, relayers, RPC access, private networking, monitoring, backup, and key custody require bank operational controls.
- The local UI starts these services for presentation convenience; this does not move validation out of the contracts.
- The local action API is bound to an exact loopback origin. Mutations require exact Host/Origin, same-origin Fetch Metadata, JSON media type and a per-session CSRF token bound to an opaque `HttpOnly`, `SameSite=Strict` cookie; rejected requests do not reach runtime execution. This protects the localhost browser boundary against cross-site request submission, not against same-origin script compromise or a compromised host.
- The evidence runner gives every child and provenance command an explicit allowlisted environment. Unsafe local mode, ADMIN/DEBUG RPC, managed profile overrides, unsupported CLI flags and process/provenance injection variables fail before isolated-runtime mutation.
- Security report v2 records a forced clean compilation and structured Hardhat counts for each exact source/signature pair. Summary v3 records a checksummed effective profile, source/tool provenance, substantive integration/validator-availability evidence and checksums of the four public component reports.
- The evidence runner holds `.runtime/locks/institutional-evidence.lock` for the complete run; security report generation also holds `.runtime/locks/security-scenarios.lock`. Initial and completion provenance must match, including safe Git index flags. Lock ownership is checked during release; after a crash, the next local run reclaims only a valid same-host/same-platform record whose PID the OS confirms is absent. Age, foreign ownership and unverifiable process state never authorize reclamation.
- Attestor private keys are written below `.runtime/besu-qbft-evidence/private/`, classified as secret and excluded from the `.runtime/evidence/` public report allowlist.
- The offline verifier requires the exact five-file public bundle, exact chain/contract bytecode inventory and no remaining managed lock. It marks otherwise passed evidence `stale` when it is not applicable to the current clean commit. Failed integrity and `stale` applicability are both `NOT READY` conditions.
- Repository regression controls include byte-exact regeneration of an offline EIP-1186-compatible corpus, four bounded stateful lending properties, fresh line-coverage thresholds and a four-mutant isolated smoke gate. They improve test sensitivity but do not become on-chain guarantees, live-client observations or formal verification.

## Availability and Failure Semantics

- One of four validators may stop while the remaining three retain QBFT block production.
- Two stopped validators remove quorum and must halt progress safely.
- Fewer than three available attestors prevent new checkpoints and settlement but do not authorize invalid execution.
- Relayer outage delays settlement; restart resumes durable jobs and reconciles transactions that may have mined before the crash.
- Acknowledgement proves completion. After timeout, a destination receipt-absence proof is required before compensation.
- Gateway pause blocks new sends and destination receives, while proof-checked acknowledgement and timeout transitions remain available so already proof-authorized work can reach a terminal state.
- Lending emergency pause is deliberately asymmetric: new borrowing and risk-increasing withdrawals stop, while repayment and collateral top-up remain available. Liquidity supply, liquidation and bad-debt absorption have independent flags so governance can isolate their operational risk.
- Interest older than one year is retained as explicit backlog. Keepers process bounded one-year batches; actions that would operate with more than one batch outstanding fail closed rather than silently granting a debt holiday.
- A written-off borrower is marked defaulted even when reserves absorb the loss. New origination remains blocked until policy governance records a nonzero credit-resolution reference.
- If escrow, voucher, or identity policy prevents a compensation callback, the whole timeout transaction reverts atomically and no partial accounting transition is committed. A suspended account remains `Pending` and may retry after remediation. If the original sender is terminally revoked, timeout compensation is routed to the configured institutional restitution vault, recorded against the message ID, and released only after governed adjudication to a currently eligible, policy-approved recipient.
- A live, foreign-host or unverifiable managed evidence/security-report lock halts its protected operation and makes verification fail. A same-host restart recovers automatically only after the OS confirms the recorded PID is absent; age-based guessing is forbidden, so a slow owner cannot be displaced merely because its lock is old.
- The UI listener is acquired before embedded-runtime initialization. Startup and idempotent shutdown attempt cleanup of every owned component; static asset open/read failures and disconnected clients are contained to the affected response.
- Runtime shutdown waits for tracked actions and relay work before releasing journal locks and providers. A process crash can leave a journal lock; restart automatically reclaims it only when a valid same-host, same-platform record names a PID that the OS confirms is absent. Live, foreign-platform and unverifiable owners remain fail-closed, and the preserved journal is reconciled before new financial work.

## Known Limitations

- Consortium-trusted 3-of-4 attestor model rather than source-validator trust minimization.
- Local deterministic keys, loopback RPC, short governance delay, and manual oracle are defense-profile choices only. Generated validator keys are isolated per container but are not HSM-backed credentials.
- No external smart-contract audit, formal verification, privacy review, or regulatory certification.
- The MPT differential harness is an independently implemented in-repository reference. The additional pinned corpus is deterministically generated offline by `@ethereumjs/trie@6.2.1` and is EIP-1186-compatible, but its metadata remains `validatedLiveClients=[]`. Integration v3 can separately evidence one live Besu family with four production-accepted proof observations; comparison against Geth, Nethermind or another independently implemented live client remains desirable before external audit.
- Stateful exploration is bounded to 12 lending actions at 64 runs × depth 64, and its debt-share property permits 10,000 wei of aggregate rounding drift. LCOV gates line coverage rather than branch coverage. The `4/4` mutation result covers only four hand-selected security regressions, not the mutation space of the full repository.
- No production HSM/mTLS deployment, shared relay database, multisig governance, or disaster-recovery exercise.
- The named evidence scenario recreates the relay engine and journal in the same process. It validates persisted-state reconciliation but does not start a fresh OS process with a new provider/key lifecycle.
- The local exact-raw outbox is not business-level exactly-once for independent external clients, and its retained request history has no production archival/compaction policy. Recovery tests inject transaction crash windows, reopen journals and kill a child process to validate orphan-lock recovery; they do not constitute a complete host disaster-recovery exercise. A permanently irreconcilable action remains globally fenced and backs off to periodic retries because this prototype has no operator quarantine/override workflow. Multi-process operation requires a transactional database with CAS/fencing rather than shared JSON files.
- Single 18-decimal collateral/debt market with simplified, discrete-compounding interest and institutional liquidation economics. Tokens with other decimals are rejected rather than normalized. Origination-principal limits are intentionally not accrued-exposure limits; production risk systems should monitor both accrued debt and principal capacity.
- Crash-fault validator evidence exists; a complete Byzantine validator/attestor adversarial campaign does not.
- Restitution resolves on-chain custody continuity, not the off-chain legal entitlement decision; production adjudication, case retention and appeal procedures remain institutional responsibilities.
- Performance observations from one local machine are not production throughput or latency claims.
- Readiness signals are recent observations with bounded staleness, not a guarantee of future availability or a production SLA.
- Evidence SHA-256 values are consistency checks, not signatures or proof-of-execution attestations. Host/toolchain compromise, administrator collusion and fabrication of an entire evidence directory remain outside the demonstrated threat boundary.
- Dirty-tree `calibration-passed` output is never evidence-eligible; a clean passed bundle becomes stale when the current tree is dirty, source-mismatched or source-unknown and must not be presented as current.
- A crashed evidence runner can leave a lock requiring operator diagnosis; the pipeline deliberately favors fail-closed exclusivity over automatic stale-lock availability recovery.
